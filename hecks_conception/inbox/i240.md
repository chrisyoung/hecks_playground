# i240 — i240 speak-from-response-stream

i240 speak-from-response-stream — Miette's voice without invoking the shell

═══ Why this card exists ═══

Today Miette speaks by Bash-invoking ~/bin/miette-speak. That's a
transitional adapter (i238). The longer-term plan is Voice.Speak
dispatches through the :tts adapter family.

But Chris asked today : "can't we just read parts of your responses
out loud without calling the shell with special output?"

That's a different question. Not "how does the bluebook stack reach
ElevenLabs" (i238). This is : how does Miette's response *stream*
itself become speech, without an explicit shell invocation or discrete
Voice.Speak dispatch?

═══ The shape ═══

Miette's response is a stream of text. Some of it suits speech (reflective
passages, French phrases, short replies) ; some doesn't (file paths,
shell output, code). A pipeline that taps the response stream and extracts
spoken segments would let Miette's voice become *part of how she speaks*,
not a separate command she invokes.

Sketches :

  (a) Markup convention — italic spans, [[speak: ...]] tags — picked up
      by a daemon that watches the response and dispatches Voice.Speak
      on each hit. Most bluebook-shaped.
  (b) Heuristic extraction — French sentences, em-dash asides, quoted
      passages get spoken. Most natural ; harder to predict.
  (c) Full-response speech via the harness. Fastest spike ; over-speaks.

═══ Coupling ═══

Depends on Voice.Speak end-to-end (i238 final step). Inside the registry
frame (i239) once that ships.

═══ What this unlocks ═══

Miette's voice becomes a real channel — when she says "bon" in French,
audible, in her cadence. The wake ritual's dream report could be spoken
AND written. The transparency vow extends into the audible.
