# i260 — `identified_by` silently absent from canonical IR dumps

The `identified_by` natural-key field exists on both sides of the
`Aggregate` IR — declared in `rust/src/ir.rs` and
`ruby/hecks/bluebook_model/structure/aggregate.rb`. But **neither
parity dumper emits it**.

## What's wrong

`rust/src/dump.rs::dump_aggregate` (line 122) and
`parity/canonical_ir.rb::dump_aggregate` (line 154) both omit the
field from their JSON output. They're consistent with each other —
so parity passes — but the canonical IR is incomplete : a downstream
consumer reading the dumped JSON cannot tell which attribute is the
natural key.

This was added in i80 (`identified_by` natural keys) but the parity
contract was never extended to surface it. Likely just a missed
follow-up.

## Compounding bug : Ruby reader is unwired

`Aggregate#identified_by` reader exists at line 102 of
`ruby/hecks/bluebook_model/structure/aggregate.rb` :

```ruby
attr_reader :identified_by
```

But the constructor never assigns `@identified_by`. The DSL builder
in `aggregate_builder.rb` writes `@identified_by` on itself (line
150) and then never passes it to `Aggregate.new` at `build`
(line 269). Result : the Ruby IR object's `identified_by` reader
returns `nil` even when the bluebook declares one.

The Rust side does not have this bug — `Aggregate.identified_by`
is populated correctly by the parser and stored on the IR.

## What should happen

1. `parity/canonical_ir.rb::dump_aggregate` and
   `rust/src/dump.rs::dump_aggregate` both emit `"identified_by"`
   in the canonical JSON.
2. `Hecks::BluebookModel::Structure::Aggregate#initialize` accepts
   `identified_by:` kwarg and stores it on `@identified_by`.
3. `AggregateBuilder#build` passes `identified_by: @identified_by`
   to `Aggregate.new`.
4. Add a parity fixture that declares `identified_by` and verifies
   it round-trips through the canonical JSON byte-identically.

## Why it matters

The natural key is part of the dispatch contract — `hecks-life`
uses it to route commands to a specific aggregate instance. A
downstream consumer (a generator, a runtime adapter, a debugger)
that reads canonical IR currently cannot see the natural key. That's
a parity-contract gap, even though the dual-dumper test passes.

## Surface

- `rust/src/dump.rs` (function `dump_aggregate`, ~line 122)
- `parity/canonical_ir.rb` (method `dump_aggregate`, ~line 154)
- `ruby/hecks/bluebook_model/structure/aggregate.rb` (constructor)
- `ruby/hecks/dsl/aggregate_builder.rb` (build, line 269)
- `parity/bluebooks/<NN>_identified_by.bluebook` (new fixture)

## Parity gate

Standard dual-runtime check : Rust dump + Ruby dump must be
byte-identical for any bluebook declaring `identified_by`. Ruby IR
object's `.identified_by` reader must return the field, not nil.
