# i624 - CorrespondenceDiscipline macrophage : every AI-generated correspondence flows through Writing.Compose first

## 1. The verbatim quote

> "we need a macrophage that checks the responses from ai to make sure correspondence goes through the writing bluebook"

Said 2026-05-15. The Writing bluebook (i623) had just landed earlier in the day with the `Tools::Writing::Writing.Compose` surface and the `WritingDiscipline` macrophage that scans composed text for the em-dash pattern and the false-contrast collective-voice pattern. The gap Chris named in this directive is one layer up : nothing today FORCES the prose through `Writing.Compose` before it leaves the system. WritingDiscipline checks what is composed ; this card asks for the sibling that checks whether composition happened at all.

---

## 2. The gap

i623 gave Writing a place to live and a discipline to enforce on its content. But the bus does not yet require correspondence to pass through it. Specifically :

- `Tools::EmailTool.CreateDraft` can be dispatched with any `draft_text` regardless of whether the text was first run through `Writing.Compose`.
- The native `mcp__claude_ai_Gmail__create_draft` MCP tool can be invoked directly, bypassing StoreHouse entirely until i599's PreToolUse auto-route hook covers it.
- Future correspondence aggregates (SlackTool, SmsTool, anything in a Comms category) inherit the same blind spot by default.

So a perfectly clean Compose-then-send flow and a malicious-or-careless direct-send flow look identical to the macrophage family today. WritingDiscipline scans content but cannot tell whether the content passed through it ; the audit trail is incomplete by construction.

---

## 3. The rule

`correspondence_without_compose` - for every dispatch that emits AI-generated correspondence (the watched surface list in section 4), the macrophage requires a preceding `Tools::Writing::Writing.Compose` dispatch with matching content within the configured window.

Concrete trigger conditions :

1. The bus observes a dispatch to a watched correspondence surface.
2. The macrophage scans the recent dispatch history (default window : 5 minutes ; configurable via `SetWindow`).
3. Within that window, if no `Tools::Writing::Writing.Compose` dispatch exists whose `draft_text` matches (per section 5) the outgoing correspondence body, the macrophage records a `FlowViolation` and dispatches `Complain`.

If the watched surface appears in the `ExemptedSurface` collection, the macrophage records the dispatch but skips Complain. Exemptions are per-surface and require a non-empty `ReasonText` (same shape as AntibodyExemption and IdentityDiscipline).

## 5. Matching semantics

Three options were considered for comparing the `Writing.Compose` `draft_text` against the correspondence body :

1. **Exact byte match** - the correspondence body is identical to the composed draft. Strictest ; brittle. A trailing newline or a trailing signature would cause a false complaint.
2. **Substring match** - the composed draft appears as a contiguous substring of the correspondence body. Tolerates appended signatures, leading greetings inserted by the tool, and small post-Compose edits.
3. **Hash equivalence** - both texts are hashed (SHA-256 of normalized whitespace) and the hashes must match. Robust against whitespace drift but breaks under any content edit.

**Call : substring match for the seed.** Reasons :

- Real composed text often gets a signature, a thread quote, or a `From:` header appended before send. Exact match falsely fires on every one of those.
- Hash equivalence is no more accurate than exact match for partial-edit cases ; it just normalizes whitespace.
- Substring match is the simplest rule that captures the intent : "the composed prose IS the correspondence body, modulo wrappers."
- If substring proves too lax (Miette starts composing one sentence and writing five), the rule can tighten to a minimum-substring-ratio check (e.g. composed text must cover at least 80 percent of the correspondence body) without changing the bluebook contract.

The `MatchKind` VO carries the chosen mode (default `substring` ; alternatives `exact` and `hash_normalized`) so per-surface overrides are possible without a code change.

## 6. Complaint format

The complaint follows the established voice of the per-rule sibling family. Template :

```
The CorrespondenceDiscipline macrophage expected Writing.Compose to dispatch before <CorrespondenceCommand>#<id>. None was found in the prior <window> minutes. Correspondence sent without composing through the bluebook misses the style rules WritingDiscipline enforces and the cumulative audit trail.
```

Filled example :

```
The CorrespondenceDiscipline macrophage expected Writing.Compose to dispatch before Tools::EmailTool.CreateDraft#cmd_a91f. None was found in the prior 5 minutes. Correspondence sent without composing through the bluebook misses the style rules WritingDiscipline enforces and the cumulative audit trail.
```

Surface : stderr from the hook, picked up by Claude Code as `additionalContext` in the next turn. Identical mechanism to BluebookFirst and IdentityDiscipline.

## 8. Acceptance

This card is closed when :

1. The `CorrespondenceDiscipline` aggregate exists in `macrophage.bluebook` with `StartSession`, `Check`, `RecordViolation`, `Complain`, `ExemptSurface`, `RevokeExemption`, `WatchSurface`, `UnwatchSurface`, `SetWindow`.
2. The seed watched-surface list covers the five live entries in section 4.
3. The hook dispatches `CorrespondenceDiscipline.Check` for every correspondence-surface dispatch on the bus.
4. When no preceding `Writing.Compose` is found inside the window, `Complain` fires and the complaint text matches the format in section 6.
5. The complaint surfaces as `additionalContext` on the next turn (same shape as the bluebook-first macrophage today).
6. An `ExemptedSurface` entry suppresses complaints for that surface while preserving the `FlowViolation` audit trail.
7. `macrophage.behaviors` carries placeholder coverage for `StartSession`, `Check`, `RecordViolation`, `Complain`, `ExemptSurface`.

## 9. Open questions for Chris

- **The 5-minute window** - is that the right default ? A long-form drafting session might compose at minute 0 and send at minute 8. If the macrophage starts false-firing on legitimate slow drafts, raise the default to 15 minutes. Configurable per the `SetWindow` command in any case.
- **Hash-normalized as the eventual default** ? If trailing-signature drift turns out to be the dominant edit pattern, hash on whitespace-normalized text might beat substring. Substring is the seed ; the VO is in place to switch.
- **Should the macrophage also fire on EMPTY correspondence ?** A `CreateDraft` with an empty `draft_text` is a different shape of violation - probably worth a separate `ViolationKind` (`empty_correspondence_body`) rather than folding into `correspondence_without_compose`.
- **Future: Cascade.RecordResult sweep** - once StoreHouse logs every dispatch (i622), the macrophage could batch-verify a whole session's worth of correspondence flows at session close, not just per-dispatch. Out of scope for the seed.
