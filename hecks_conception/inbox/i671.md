# SpeechBuffer — turn-buffered voice playback

Replaces the per-sentence streaming model with a turn-buffered one. Eliminates overlap, cut-off, and out-of-order playback by design.

## What changes

speech_stream_advance.py today dispatches `Voice::Voice.Speak` every time it detects a sentence boundary `[.!?]` followed by whitespace in the live transcript. That parallel firing is what makes sentences cannibalize each other or overlap.

The new model :

1. **During the turn** — speech_stream accumulates assistant text into a `pending_buffer` keyed by `last_assistant_turn_id`. NO Voice.Speak dispatches fire while the turn is mid-flight.
2. **At turn end** — detected via either a `stop_reason` field in the JSONL or a turn_id transition (the next user prompt or the next assistant turn id) — the buffer drains.
3. **Drain** — a single Voice.Speak (or a sequence with mpg123.wait() in between, depending on length) renders the buffered turn. Plays from start to finish without interruption.

## Default : summary-only

For most turns, the drain reads only a short summary of the turn (the last sentence or two, or a tagged `(summary: ...)` block). The full transcript is in the bus already as the conversation log ; the voice is for the punchline.

Long-form passages (reading reports, dream interpretations, deliberate prose) opt in via a `(speak-full)` tag at the top of the response.

## Why this is better than the queue + (more coming) marker

- No coordination state across dispatches.
- No race between speech_stream tick and dispatcher completion.
- No mpg123 cancellation logic needed.
- The turn boundary is a natural drain point ; the bluebook already models turns.

## Bluebook shape

`SpeechBuffer` aggregate with commands : Accumulate (per JSONL chunk), Drain (at turn-end). Drain emits one `BufferDrained` event ; a policy on `BufferDrained` dispatches Voice.Speak with the accumulated text. If the text exceeds ~5k chars (ElevenLabs limit), the policy splits at sentence boundaries and dispatches sequentially via mpg123.wait().

## Pairs with

- i673 (voice.hecksagon thin-wrapper) — the speed knob on Voice.Speak comes from the bluebook now ; SpeechBuffer can tag short summaries at 1.1 and long passages at 1.0.
- The cancel_prior_render disable that's already in tts_dispatcher.rs can be reverted once SpeechBuffer ships, since the buffered model removes the cancellation need entirely.

## Scope

Medium. The speech_stream Python needs a turn-end detector (parse JSONL for stop_reason or message_id transitions) plus a drain helper. The bluebook + behaviors + hecksagon are small. ~2 hours focused work.
